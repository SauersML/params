AlgZoo challenge model M16,10 (example_2nd_argmax, ARC's 432-parameter model, not part of the zoo).
Task: position of the second largest of 10 independent standard Gaussian numbers. Architecture: 16 ReLU neurons,
h_(t+1) = ReLU(W_hh h_t + W_hi x_t), logits = W_oh h_10, no biases; 432 parameters.
Reference accuracy 0.953097 +- 3.6e-05 (exact probability along the last input, averaged over Gaussian prefixes; this is sampling, used only as the truth).

ARC'S CRITERION: (a) a mechanistic estimate of the accuracy (deduced from the weights, not from sampled inputs)
whose error over noisy copies or seeds is no worse than random sampling's at the same operation count, at every
budget; (b) full understanding: the explanation's surprise ~ the bits of optimization that selected the model.

Reading of the tables (challenge model):
  - Records: neuron 4 is on exactly when the newest input is a new maximum (98.5% from its rank alone), and
    neuron 7's pre-activation is minus neuron 4's (correlation -1.00 over steps 3-10): the pair holds x_new - M_old as a signed
    number. Neuron 2 is always on and holds the running maximum one step late (ARC's description).
  - Neurons 1, 6, 8 (leave-one-out maxima minus the newest input, per ARC and Chen) switch on the newest
    input's rank (95-97%).
  - A record clock: one step after a new maximum neurons 5 and 9 are on; two steps after, neuron 0 is off;
    five steps after, neuron 11 is off; six steps after, neuron 10 is off. Ages 1, 2, 5 and 6 each have a
    marker neuron; ages 3, 4 and 7-9 have none, so there the age must sit in the neurons' values (decision
    trees on ranks predict 0, 10, 11 at 98-99%).
  - Neurons 3 and 15 follow value comparisons, not ranks (89-92% from ranks; they change most when values
    are redrawn with the ordering fixed).

WHY THIS DOES NOT YET GIVE A MECHANISTIC ESTIMATE
  - Correctness is not a function of the ordering: redrawing the values with the ordering of (x, 0) fixed flips
    correctness on 0.073 of inputs; of the errors only 23% persist; the activation pattern changes on 96% of inputs
    (4.8 neuron-steps on average). The model compares values with tilted weights.
  - Nor of the top three values and positions: redrawing the other seven flips 0.033 of inputs (64% of errors persist).
  - Exact integration along the last input (lastline.py) cuts the per-input variance from 0.0450 to 0.0208; over the last two
    and three inputs to 0.0125 and 0.0092, at a cost that multiplies with each input. The remaining 7-9 dimensions need a point set.
  - Activation patterns (challenge model, 1048576 inputs): 555138 distinct; 43% of the probability is on patterns never
    seen (Good-Turing); the 100,000 commonest cover 56%. Cone enumeration is out of reach.
  - One canonical point per ordering of the first nine inputs (all 9! orderings, median order statistics,
    exact last input; aqn.py) gives 0.990 against 0.953, at the cost of 483013 inputs: the
    errors live at small gaps that the canonical points miss.
  - ARC's five features fit their neurons (R^2 0.94-0.995) but substituting them drops accuracy to 0.31 (all five)
    or 0.37-0.93 (one at a time); they are exact at steps 1-4 and fail at 6-10 (local agent, m16_features.py).
  - Weight noise: mean accuracy of copies sd 0.002: 0.921 (+- 0.029), sd 0.005: 0.786 (+- 0.070), sd 0.05: 0.197 (+- 0.049) against 0.953.

SCORE
  (a) Error against compute: NOT MET by a mechanistic estimator. The only mechanistic estimate here, 'the model
      computes the task' (1.0), has RMS 0.086 over the six trained models and beats sampling only while
      sampling has fewer than 10 inputs (52794 ops). The deterministic Halton + exact-line estimator
      is not mechanistic; its rows above show what the exact line integration buys over plain sampling:
      trained: beats sampling at N = [8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384], loses at N = [1, 2, 4]; RMS ratio 0.74-0.95 from N = 1024 up.
      noisy_sd0.002: beats sampling at N = [16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384], loses at N = [1, 2, 4, 8]; RMS ratio 0.56-0.72 from N = 1024 up.
      noisy_sd0.005: beats sampling at N = [512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384], loses at N = [1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256]; RMS ratio 0.44-0.74 from N = 1024 up.
  What the noisy-copies protocol does not test (m16_taylor.py): advice made of the original accuracy, the
  gradient of accuracy with respect to the 432 weights and one mean curvature term (434 numbers, about |theta|,
  computed once by sampling) gives a + g.(theta' - theta) + c for each copy at 1298 ops (a quarter of one input). It explains nothing.
  On the sd 0.002 copies its RMS is 0.0195 (the original accuracy alone: 0.0387), which sampling needs 187 inputs to match.
  On the sd 0.005 copies its RMS is 0.0624 (the original accuracy alone: 0.1685), which sampling needs 40 inputs to match.
  Passing the noisy protocol at low budgets is therefore weak evidence of understanding; the seeds protocol
  (no shared original model) is the stronger test.
  (b) Surprise accounting: NOT MET. There is no compact explanation: the partial algorithm above leaves the
      second maximum's position code (spread over 13 neurons) and the value-dependent neurons unexplained,
      and the accuracy depends on fine weight details (noise of sd 0.002 on every weight costs 3.2 points).
